In the remote wilderness surrounding an abandoned government research complex known as D-756, four middle-aged contract workers, each carrying personal regrets and unresolved issues from their past, are hired for a six-week assignment. Their responsibilities include inventorying outdated servers, erasing stored data, and modernizing the facility's technological infrastructure before its decommissioning.

The team consists of Sam (Kevin McCorkle), an eccentric IT specialist with a tendency to recognize patterns; Michaels (Michael Peter Bolus), a sarcastic former convict with a dark sense of humor; O'Neel (Michael Town), a knowledgeable geologist struggling with family estrangement; and Mac (Greg McGowen), the reluctant mission coordinator still dealing with the consequences of a violent demotion.

The men arrive after a turbulent flight for which they are provided with anti-anxiety medication. Completely isolated from civilization, they have no cellular service, no internet access, and limited supplies. During their evenings together, they discuss military experiences, conspiracy theories, and personal matters.

After restoring the facility's communication equipment, the team intercepts a mysterious numbers station broadcasting a recurring sequence of sixteen numbers. The transmission appears to encode geographical coordinates pointing to a location near the facility's hangar.

Meanwhile, Mac discovers archived recordings featuring Dr. Louise Caldwell, apparently connected to a previous team that disappeared under unexplained circumstances. Rather than informing his colleagues, Mac conceals his discovery.

As the assignment progresses, the workers encounter an increasing number of disturbing inconsistencies. Despite the facility's supposed ten-year period of inactivity, some of its equipment appears unexpectedly modern. Michaels and O'Neel discover recently burned, bloodstained clothing inside a burn barrel. Other unsettling discoveries include unexplained blood spatter and the crushed carcass of a wolf that scavengers refuse to approach.

A persistent low-frequency hum permeates the complex, seemingly resonating inside the men's heads. They begin experiencing auditory and visual hallucinations, including whispers in empty rooms, red glows behind closed doors, unnatural moving shadows, and disturbing visions connected to their personal histories.

The radio transmission gradually changes, introducing new numerical sequences that appear to count down toward the present day. Simultaneously, the facility exhibits increasingly unusual behavior: doors lock or slam without apparent intervention, lights flash erratically, and objects inexplicably appear or disappear.

As the days pass, the men's psychological conditions deteriorate. Their memories become fragmented, and their hallucinations increasingly force them to confront buried guilt, regrets, and personal secrets. Ordinary disagreements escalate into mutual distrust, paranoia, and violence. Each man begins to suspect that his colleagues, or an unknown presence within or beneath the facility, possess knowledge of his past.

The accumulated discoveries suggest that D-756 may be more than an abandoned Cold War-era government research site. Rather than performing a routine technological assignment, the workers may themselves be unwitting participants in a concealed government experiment.

As the distinction between external phenomena and psychological breakdown becomes increasingly unclear, the group descends into conflict and individual instability. The isolation, unexplained discoveries, and resurfacing personal traumas gradually destroy the trust between the four men.

The film concludes with a signature on an orientation form, bringing the narrative full circle and implying that the assignment was never truly concerned with technological maintenance. The exact nature of the experiment, the origin of the disturbing phenomena, and the extent to which the men's experiences were externally manipulated remain ambiguous.
